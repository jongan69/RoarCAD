RoarCAD vs Flux.ai vs Quilter

These products overlap, but they solve different jobs. Flux is broad browser ECAD, Quilter automates physical layout, and RoarCAD focuses on inspectable agent changes, immutable review handoff, and human approval.

ProductBest fitStarts withDistinct strengthImportant boundary
RoarCADSmall teams that want a transparent agent and revision workflowRequirements or a structured BoardGraphFour bounded WebMCP tools, non-mutating previews, human-only apply, tamper-evident checkpointsNot a proven replacement for professional ECAD or physical verification
Flux.aiTeams wanting a collaborative, end-to-end browser ECADAn idea, requirements, or an editable projectPlanning, schematic/BOM work, layout, manufacturing output, component data, and real-time collaborationA broader commercial product with subscription and AI-credit pricing
QuilterEngineers automating placement and routing of an existing designA prepared schematic, board file, and constraintsMultiple physics-driven candidates, checks, and native ECAD return filesIt is primarily layout automation, not requirements-to-schematic collaboration

Choose by the bottleneck

  • Choose RoarCAD when the main risk is trusting and handing off agent-made changes.
  • Choose Flux when you want a mature browser design environment with collaboration across the broader PCB workflow.
  • Choose Quilter when you already have the electrical design and need to compress placement and routing time.

What this comparison does not claim

RoarCAD does not claim feature parity with Flux, physics-validation parity with Quilter, or evidence that PocketRoar is ready to fabricate. Competitor descriptions come from their official product pages and may change; follow the linked sources for current details.

RoarCAD’s differentiator: the agent has useful tools but no tool that can approve its own change. A person sees the diff and creates the next immutable revision.
